Floe Chime: Penguin Puzzle

Privacy Policy · Effective date: 12 November 2026

The short version. Floe Chime has no accounts, no sign-in and no servers of its own. Your progress, lives, coins, daily streak and settings are kept on your phone. The developer never receives your name, email address or payment details.

Four services are involved: Google Play handles purchases, Google AdMob shows the game's ads, Google's Firebase (Analytics and Crashlytics) tells the developer, in anonymous numbers, how the game is played and when it crashes, and, only where Google's consent tool says consent is not required, TikTok's measurement SDK tells TikTok whether its ads led to installs, progress and purchases. Each is described below, along with how to switch it off.

1. Who is responsible

Floe Chime is made and published by an independent developer. Questions about this policy, or about data the game handles, go to sonictradingteknoloji@gmail.com.

2. Who the game is for

Floe Chime is for players aged 13 and over. It is not aimed at children, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. It contains third-party advertising, limited to content rated suitable for general audiences (G).

3. What the game keeps on your phone

The following is saved in the game's private storage on your phone. The developer does not receive it:

  • Which levels you have cleared, and the level you are playing now, so you can pick up where you left off
  • Your lives and when the next one arrives
  • Your coins, Undos and ice picks
  • Your Daily streak and results
  • Which hints you have used
  • Your settings: Sound, Haptics, Tap to preview, and your choice on "Share usage, crash and ad measurement data"
  • Simple ad counters: how many ads you have watched for a life today, and how recently an ad was shown, so the game can keep ads infrequent
  • A record of the purchases you made, so you never lose what you bought

None of this identifies you. Deleting the game removes it from your phone. Backups are covered in section 8.

When you tap Share on a Daily result, the game hands a short line of text to Android's share sheet. Where it goes from there is your choice; the game itself sends nothing.

4. Advertising (Google AdMob and Google's consent tool)

Floe Chime shows two kinds of ads, both delivered by Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK. Every ad request is for non-personalised ads only, limited to content rated G.

Ads you choose (rewarded ads)

A rewarded ad plays only after you tap a button that offers something in return: Watch an ad for one extra life when you are out of lives (up to five times a day), or Watch an ad: +2 fish on a level you have just lost (from level 21 on, once per try, never in the Daily). It never starts on its own, and no part of the game depends on watching one.

Ads between levels

From level 21 on, an ad may appear after you win a level, when you move on to the next one. They are kept infrequent: at most one every three wins, at least three minutes apart, never during play, never after a loss, never on the first win of a session, never at the end of a sector, and never in the Daily. There are no ads between levels, and no Watch an ad: +2 fish offer, in the first 20 levels. The only ad you can meet before level 21 is the optional Watch an ad for a life, if you run out of lives and choose it. Remove Ads or the Supporter Pack turns the ads between levels off; the optional rewarded ads stay.

What Google receives when an ad is requested

To choose and show an ad, measure it and prevent fraud, Google receives information such as your IP address (from which an approximate location can be worked out), your phone's advertising ID, device and app details (model, Android version, app version), whether the ad was shown, watched or closed, and diagnostic information about ad delivery. Because the ads are non-personalised, this is used for things like limiting how often you see an ad, measurement and fraud prevention, not to build an advertising profile of you from the game.

Google handles this data under its own terms: see Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

Where Google's User Messaging Platform says consent is required (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), a Google consent form is shown before any ad is requested: the first time you tap a button that offers an ad, or at a quiet moment after you finish level 19, never in the middle of a level. Your answer is remembered. You can change it at any time in Settings → Privacy Choices, which appears there only when Google's tool says it applies to you.

5. Usage and crash data (Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics)

Floe Chime uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, both from Google, to learn which levels are too hard or too easy and to fix crashes.

What is collected

  • Gameplay events, as numbers and fixed names only, never free text: a level started, won or failed (with the level number, sector, and how many fish were left or taps used); a Hint, Undo or Ice pick used, or refused because there weren't enough coins; the "No way out" panel shown; a rewarded-ad offer shown or taken; a chance for an ad between levels and what came of it (shown, none loaded, failed to show, or a rule said no); an ad that failed to load (a numeric error code); a "Watch an ad" tap that found no ad; the Store opened; a purchase started (which product); a Daily started, won or shared, and your streak milestones.
  • Purchases are counted by Firebase's automatic purchase event (Google Play is linked to Firebase); the game does not log them itself.
  • Crash reports (Crashlytics): what the game was doing when it crashed (stack traces and app state), device details such as model and Android version, and random installation IDs that Crashlytics and Firebase give this install.
  • The advertising ID is collected where you have allowed it, for measuring installs and purchases only, never for targeting. The Android ID (SSAID) is not collected.

The game does not set a user ID, user properties, or custom Crashlytics notes, and it never asks for your name or email. Google also receives technical information any internet connection carries, such as your IP address. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and Firebase's privacy information.

No personalised advertising

Ad personalisation signals are switched off for the game's Firebase data. The advertising ID is used only so the developer can see whether ads for the game lead to installs and purchases through Google Ads measurement. It is not used to show you personalised ads.

When collection is on, and how consent works

Analytics and Crashlytics are off on a new install until the game has decided. The game switches them on only if you have not turned them off and Google's consent tool allows it. After that, your phone remembers the last decision, and the game checks it again every time it opens, as soon as Google's consent tool answers, switching collection off at once if your choice or your consent has changed.

  • Outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland: it is on by default, from the first launch once Google's tool has answered (a first launch with no internet sends nothing).
  • Inside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland: nothing is collected until you agree in Google's consent form. If you later withdraw consent in Settings → Privacy Choices, collection stops again.
  • A crash that happens before consent stays on your phone, and is sent only if consent arrives. If you refuse analytics consent, or switch the setting off, crash reports that were waiting are deleted.

How to opt out

  • Everyone: open Settings and turn off Share usage, crash and ad measurement data. This stops Analytics and Crashlytics whatever any consent form says, deletes crash reports that have not been sent, and stops the ad measurement in section 5a (with the one limit stated there). It is on by default, and it is saved on your phone only.
  • EEA, UK and Switzerland (wherever Google's consent tool applies): you can also change your answer in Settings → Privacy Choices.
  • Everywhere: reset or delete your advertising ID (section 7), or uninstall the game.

How long it is kept, and deletion requests

Analytics data is kept for 2 months and then deleted automatically. Crash reports are kept for as long as Crashlytics' own retention allows, which Google sets. Because the game sets no user ID and never shows you its Firebase installation ID, the developer cannot tell which data came from which person, so a request to delete one person's data cannot be carried out. The retention periods above, uninstalling, and the opt-outs are how data goes away.

5a. Ad measurement (TikTok Business SDK)

The developer may advertise the game on TikTok. To learn whether those ads work, the game contains TikTok's measurement SDK (TikTok Business SDK, version 1.7.1). It tells TikTok, and only TikTok, when someone who may have come from a TikTok ad installs the game, plays it, reaches a milestone or buys something. TikTok is a separate company with its own privacy policy: TikTok's Privacy Policy.

When it runs

  • Only where Google's consent tool says consent is not required for you (for example the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand), and only once the game has got that answer on this launch. Never in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, even if you agree in Google's consent form: there TikTok's SDK is not started and nothing is sent to TikTok.
  • Never with the Settings switch off. Settings → Share usage, crash and ad measurement data is on by default.
  • With no internet, or before Google's tool has answered, nothing is sent.

What TikTok receives

  • Events the SDK sends by itself: that the game was installed, that it was opened, and that it was opened again two days after the install.
  • Events the game sends: that you cleared level 10 (the end of the tutorial), that you cleared level 20, and that you made an in-app purchase: the price and currency Google Play showed you, a product code (a fixed short name for what was bought), and a number made from the purchase by a one-way scramble, so the same purchase is not counted twice. The purchase token itself is never sent. Each of these is sent once, the first time, and only for purchases made after the game started measuring on your phone (not for ones restored on a new phone). No ad views and no Daily results are sent. The game sends no name, email, phone number, user ID or free text.
  • What the SDK adds itself, as any ad measurement SDK does: your phone's advertising ID, an anonymous ID the SDK creates for this install, a session ID, your IP address (from which an approximate location can be worked out), your browser-style user agent text (phone model and Android version as Android reports them), phone model, Android version, language and region, screen size, network type (Wi-Fi or mobile), the game's version and package name, which store installed it, and the Google Play install referrer (which says which ad or link led to the install). The game switches off the SDK's automatic purchase tracking, its health and crash monitoring, and its extra ad-click postback.

Why

To measure the developer's TikTok ads (did they lead to installs, to players who got past the tutorial, to purchases) and to let TikTok show those ads to people likely to enjoy the game. The game itself gives TikTok no name, email, phone number or account, but the advertising ID, the anonymous install ID and the technical details above can be used to recognise your phone, and TikTok may combine them with what it knows from elsewhere.

How to stop it

  • Turn off Settings → Share usage, crash and ad measurement data. The game stops sending its own events at once. TikTok's SDK has no stop command, so its automatic events (open and the two-day return) may still go out until you close the game and open it again; from then on it is not started at all.
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID (section 7). This stops the advertising ID being sent. The SDK's anonymous install ID is separate: resetting the advertising ID does not change it, and what does reset it is not verified, so only uninstalling the game, or switching the setting off and reopening the game, is certain to stop it.
  • Uninstall the game.

The developer cannot see TikTok's data about you, and cannot tell which person any measurement belongs to. For what TikTok does with the data it receives, and to exercise rights you may have against TikTok, see TikTok's Privacy Policy above.

6. Purchases (Google Play Billing)

The game offers optional in-app purchases: Remove Ads, the Supporter Pack, +5 Lives, and Unlimited Lives for 4 hours or 1 day. There are no subscriptions. Google Play processes every purchase. The developer never sees your card details, billing address or Google Account. The game receives only Google Play's confirmation that a purchase completed, checks it, and unlocks what you bought. It keeps a record of that confirmation on your phone.

Pending payments. Some payment methods take time to clear. While a payment is pending, nothing is unlocked and the game says "Payment pending. You'll get it once it's paid." The game checks Google Play when it opens and when you come back to it, and the purchase arrives as soon as Google Play confirms the payment.

Restore. Remove Ads and the Supporter Pack belong to the Google Account that bought them. They come back on any Android phone signed in to that account when you tap Restore Purchases (in the Store and in Settings). +5 Lives and the Unlimited Lives passes are consumables: they are not restored, and the passes work only on the phone where they were bought. Refunds and purchase history are handled by Google Play. See Google's Privacy Policy.

7. The advertising ID, and how to reset or delete it

The advertising ID is a random number Android gives each phone for advertising and measurement. Google (sections 4 and 5) and, where section 5a applies, TikTok may receive it. You can reset it (a new number) or delete it (apps get no number) in your phone's settings. On most phones this is Settings → Privacy → Ads (or Settings → Google → Ads), where you choose Delete advertising ID or Reset advertising ID. Menu names differ between phones. The game keeps working without it. You can also manage Google's ad settings at adssettings.google.com.

8. Android backup

Backup is switched on for the game. If Google's backup is turned on for your phone, it may keep a copy of the game's saved progress, and the same copy can move to a new phone when you set it up (including a direct phone-to-phone transfer). That copy includes: the levels you cleared and the level in progress, your coins, Undos and ice picks, your lives, your Daily streak, your hints used, your Sound, Haptics and Tap to preview settings, and the record of your purchases. That copy is governed by Google's privacy policy, not this one.

These things are not backed up and never leave the phone: unlimited-lives passes, the lives clock check, the ad counters, whether the consent form has been answered, your Share usage, crash and ad measurement data choice, and Google's own stored consent answer. On a new phone the game may therefore ask for consent again where it applies. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings.

9. Your choices, in one place

  • Skip the optional ads: every rewarded ad is your choice.
  • Turn off ads between levels with Remove Ads or the Supporter Pack.
  • Turn off Analytics, Crashlytics and ad measurement with Settings → Share usage, crash and ad measurement data.
  • Change your consent in Settings → Privacy Choices (where it applies).
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID (section 7).
  • Uninstall the game to erase what it saved on the phone.
